# D4 — convertible enclosure structural interfaces

[Mandatory requirement](../requirements.md#convertible-removable-enclosure): switching
between enclosed and open-air operation must be entirely by hand, without tools,
structural disassembly or dismantling frame, motion, electronics or bed mechanisms.
No panel material, active heating, ventilation or filtration system is selected here.

The primary frame must retain its intended rigidity and gravity restraints with **all
removable panels absent**. Panel stiffness/friction is excluded from the analytical
models. A panel is not a substitute for a brace or independently reviewed guard/restraint.
Hand-removability does not authorize reaching into energized or moving mechanisms.

| Candidate interface | Benefits and mechanical concerns | Required later evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Captive hand-operated quarter-turn retainers | Positive engagement and repeatable stop; floating secondary mounts can allow expansion | Hand force/access, positive retention, vibration resistance, wear, pinch points and thermal ratings; no tool-required fastener hidden behind trim |
| Hook/slot or lift-off lower support plus hand latch | Weight rests on ledge; latch retains outward motion; one datum and sliding followers avoid thermal bind | Removal sweep must clear parked heads/cables; prevent accidental drop or lift-off during operation; cleaning access under ledges |
| Magnetic location with separate positive hand retention | Fast positioning, compliant installation | Magnets alone not credited as safety restraint; temperature, adhesive/fastener retention, electrical debris and fall risk need assessment |
| Hand thumbscrew/captive knob | Simple replaceable interface and explicit clamping | Remains tool-free in actual access corridor; trapped hardware, excessive hand preload and repeated removal damage need checks |
| Snap clips | Low part count and rapid removal | Printed fatigue/creep, hidden engagement and temperature-dependent force make sole retention uncertain; replacement and inspection necessary |

Proposed functional separation: permanent braced structural bay → small accessory bosses
→ panel support/retainers. Panel removal must not loosen a structural node or rail clamp.
Use a fixed locating point plus appropriate sliding/floating followers to accommodate
panel/frame expansion; a soft gasket may seal without carrying structural shear. Actual
expansion allowance needs panel material and temperature, both still undecided.

Reserve independent volumes for (1) full motion/park envelopes in S/R/C/M12/M34/M13/M24/J,
(2) all cable and filament bends, (3) hand and panel removal sweep, (4) service-tool access
to locks, rails and belts after safe isolation, and (5) debris/cleaning access. Compare
front-opening permanent rear/side/base bracing against external side diagonals. A removable
front brace required to extract a panel violates the hand-conversion requirement even
if that brace has a convenient fastener. A large panel can be tool-free yet impractical
to lift around filament feeds; no access pass is claimed without geometry.

Electrical protection must remain effective with panels removed: fixed electronics
covers, heater-wire strain relief, protected terminals and reviewed grounding/bonding
where applicable cannot disappear with an enclosure wall. Panel attachment must not
abrade cables, expose conductive fasteners to terminals or obstruct isolation controls.
No circuit design or compliance certification is implied.

Both imperial and metric variants should reference the same accessory mounting datum
where practical; verify extra socket bulk and latch access separately. The later 2020
edition needs its own slot hardware and retention check. A common panel pattern is an
upgrade hypothesis, not a promised interchangeability feature.

Future review must examine open-air versus enclosed node/rail/electronics temperatures,
thermal gradients after panel installation/removal, retention in vibration, all-mode
clearance, convenient cleaning and one-retainer failure. Those tests require reviewed
procedures and acceptance/abort criteria before operation. Enclosure capability does
not establish fire containment, unattended readiness or authorization for active heat.
